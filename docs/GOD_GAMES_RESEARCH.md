# What god and sandbox games let you do

Research notes for Animundi: what the player can do in god games and world sandboxes, grouped by category, with the games that do it and what Animundi takes.

**Sources.** WorldBox's six power categories (World Creation, Civilizations, Creatures, Nature and Disasters, Destruction, Other) and some of its powers (lightning, tornadoes, acid rain, nukes, meteorites, plague, dragons, UFOs, divine magnet) come from [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox) and a [nature and disasters guide](https://progameguides.com/guides/how-to-use-nature-and-disaster-powers-in-worldbox-god-simulator/). Everything else comes from general knowledge of the games and should be checked before quoting a game's exact feature.

**Games covered:** WorldBox, Populous, Black & White 1–2, From Dust, The Universim, Reus, Godus, SimEarth, Spore, Dwarf Fortress (world generation and Legends mode), RimWorld (storyteller), Ages of Conflict, Plague Inc., The Powder Toy / Sandboxels, Equilinox, Niche.

**Animundi column:** ✅ in the game · ❌ out of scope. Decision: everything is in, except the Spore-style creature editor and awakening animals.

## What nobody does (Animundi's space)

- **Peoples that invent their own culture and inventions**, written as code that the engine runs (only Dwarf Fortress comes close, with generated history but fixed mechanics).
- **Nature as agents**: forests, volcanoes and seas with moods and goals, not just events the player triggers.
- **Local AI minds** for peoples, monsters and spirits, with a full procedural fallback.
- **Progress that never stops**: from stone tools to planes, rockets and beyond, with no final age.
